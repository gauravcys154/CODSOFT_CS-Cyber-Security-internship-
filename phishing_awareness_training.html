What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack where an attacker impersonates a trusted entity — a bank, colleague, delivery service, or company IT department — to trick a victim into revealing sensitive information (passwords, OTPs, card numbers) or installing malware.

Email Phishing

Mass or targeted fraudulent emails asking users to click links or open attachments.

Spear Phishing

Highly targeted attack using personal details about the victim to appear credible.

Smishing

Phishing carried out via SMS text messages, often with urgent shortened links.

Vishing

Voice-call phishing where attackers impersonate support staff or bank officials.

How to Identify Suspicious Emails & Fake Pages

  • Sender address mismatch: the display name looks legitimate, but the actual email domain is misspelled (e.g. support@paypa1.com).
  • Urgency & threats: "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" is a classic pressure tactic.
  • Generic greetings: "Dear Customer" instead of your real name.
  • Suspicious links: hovering over a link shows a different URL than the text displayed.
  • Poor grammar/formatting: legitimate organizations rarely send emails full of typos.
  • Unexpected attachments: invoices, "resumes," or ZIP files from unknown senders.
  • Fake login pages: look for HTTPS + correct domain; padlock icon alone is not proof of safety.
❌ Suspicious: http://secure-paypal.account-verify.info/login
✅ Legitimate: https://www.paypal.com/signin

Common Social Engineering Techniques

Pretexting

Attacker invents a fabricated scenario (e.g. "IT support needs your password to fix an issue").

Baiting

Offering something enticing — free downloads, USB drives left in parking lots — to lure victims.

Quid Pro Quo

Promising a benefit ("free tech support") in exchange for information or access.

Tailgating

Physically following an authorized person into a restricted area.

Authority Impersonation

Posing as a CEO, law enforcement, or vendor to pressure quick compliance.

Scarcity/Urgency

Creating a false deadline so victims act before thinking critically.

Practical Security Tips

  • Never click links or download attachments from unexpected or unknown senders.
  • Verify requests for sensitive info via a separate, trusted channel (call the company directly).
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all important accounts.
  • Check the URL carefully before entering credentials — look for typos and unusual domains.
  • Keep browsers, antivirus, and OS updated to patch known vulnerabilities.
  • Use a password manager — it won't autofill credentials on a fake look-alike site.
  • Report suspicious emails to your organization's IT/security team instead of just deleting them.
  • Hover before you click — always preview the real destination URL.

Real-World Case Studies

Google & Facebook (2013–2015) — $100M Invoice Scam

Attacker Evaldas Rimasauskas impersonated a hardware vendor and sent fake invoices, tricking employees into wiring over $100 million to fraudulent accounts over two years.

Twitter 2020 Bitcoin Scam

Attackers used vishing (phone-based social engineering) to trick Twitter employees into granting access to internal admin tools, compromising high-profile verified accounts.

Target Corporation (2013)

Attackers phished credentials from a third-party HVAC vendor, using that foothold to breach Target's network and steal 40 million customers' card details.
